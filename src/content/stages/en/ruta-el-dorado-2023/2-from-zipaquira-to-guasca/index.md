The first thing I did the next day was look for somewhere to have breakfast. I headed for the bar I’d looked up online, but it was closed. Fortunately, in the square in Zipaquirá I came across a market, so I could take a stroll, look at the local crafts and, of course, have breakfast.

![](./IMG_20231105_072310.jpg)

An <em>envuelto de choclo</em>, a sweetcorn parcel, or two, and a hot chocolate were enough to give me energy for the rest of the morning.

![](./IMG_20231105_073701.jpg)

The ride from Zipaquirá to Guasca was sheer torture. There wasn’t a single flat metre: it was all up or down.

![](./IMG_20231105_100929.jpg)

The truth is that nothing much happened that day, apart from the beginning and the end.

![](./IMG_20231105_102355.jpg)

On one of those climbs I reached the highest point of the route: 3,000 metres.

![](./IMG_20231105_123744.jpg)

Mind you, the scenery was really beautiful.

![](./IMG_20231105_124519.jpg)

And you can tell. When the kilometres fly by, it means you’re really enjoying yourself.

![](./IMG_20231105_130235.jpg)

![](./IMG_20231105_134510.jpg)

In Guatavita, just before reaching Guasca, I stopped for lunch. Food... is something I remember a lot from this trip.

![](./IMG_20231105_140938.jpg)

Once at my destination, I stayed in a small guesthouse, where I enjoyed the peace and quiet of the Colombian mountains.
