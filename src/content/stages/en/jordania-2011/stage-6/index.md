The last stage: all that was left was to make our way from the depths of the <strong>Wadi Rum</strong> desert to the tourist town of <strong>Aqaba</strong>. We woke up early, as we had to get through a very sandy area, and we were going to do it by 4x4.

![Jordan, transport](./DSCN2374.jpg)

Once past this sandy stretch we would reach a track used by the 4x4s, with a firmer, <em>rideable</em> surface. The terrain was very, very beautiful, with sand and camel grass. On either side, the mountains, and ahead, sand. This kind of terrain always caught my eye when I saw it on the Paris–Dakar, back when it was raced in Africa, and now I was pedalling across one very much like it.

![Jordan, desert](./DSCN2376.jpg)

![Jordan, pedalling through the desert](./DSCN2383.jpg)

After several kilometres on these tracks, and after I’d kept on asking to be allowed to ride on my own for a while, he tells me: off you go, and wait for us at the tree. How funny: with directions like that, in Asturias we’d be lost for sure. But curiously, as you make your way through Wadi Rum, you can see <strong>the tree</strong> in the distance. It’s the only tree there is, so special that it’s even easy to find on Google Earth or any other program with aerial photos. A special place in the middle of the desert.

![Wadi Rum, the tree](./DSCN2391.jpg)

From there to the end, nothing special. Wadi Rum came to an end and we moved onto a road, and a little further on onto the motorway (yes, in Jordan you can ride on the motorway by bike, although it’s still a bad idea). From one of the most beautiful places to ride to one of the ugliest (and most dangerous). We would ride quite a few kilometres along this hateful motorway to the city of Aqaba. It’s one of Jordan’s few coastal towns, on the Gulf of Aqaba, in the Red Sea. I don’t know what to say about this city; I think a cyclist I met recently, called Oriol, would call it <em>guirilandia</em>, tourist-land. A tourist town, private beaches, beer a metre from the water... very nice, yes, but not my style.

And that was the end of the trip, short but intense and very beautiful. I like Arab countries. A lot.
