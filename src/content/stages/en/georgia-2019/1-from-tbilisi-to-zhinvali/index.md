My first stage starts with little confidence. First, generally speaking, I don’t feel confident about the trip. My state of mind isn’t the best, and not for any particular reason. I just find it hard to think about all these days on my own. As if it were my first time! And second, the traffic chaos in <em>Tbilisi</em> scares me a bit: getting out of the city could be dangerous and it’s going to take me a good while. Although maybe this second fear is just a consequence of my state of mind.

But here I am, and it’s time to start what took so much effort to plan. I leave the hotel and follow the route I downloaded from the <a href="https://bikepacking.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">bikepacking</a> website. You can tell a lot of work has gone into it. I hardly ever end up on main roads, and I keep to small streets where the traffic is lighter. It’s also true that I spend the day going up and down, while the main road runs comfortably along the flat. But I can ride in peace, and that’s what matters. Until, at one point, it takes me far off the main road onto a dirt track that turns out to be blocked on the way down, and I have to turn back. Looking at the route again, I see it goes <em>a bit crazy</em>, so I decide to try my luck on the main road. Yes, the traffic is crazy and drivers see bikes as something that shouldn’t be there... but I ride fast and come out in one piece.

![Tbilisi](./tblisi_traffic.jpg)

The buildings disappear and I reach another busy road, which I quickly leave to head into the first ambush of the trip. The route turns off into the hills, where you can barely make out what must once have been a path. Neither the gradient nor the surface lets me pedal, so I push the bike... for far too long. The vegetation is tall and very dry (and angry!), and it clings to my bare legs, sticking me with every kind of thorn: long, short, hook-shaped... It leaves my legs looking like a cat has sharpened its claws on them. But right now what worries me most is whether the sealant in my tyres will hold; I wouldn’t like to start the trip taking out the <em>tubeless</em> setup to fit an inner tube. Did I mention the sun is merciless? Not a scrap of shade, of course.

Once this climb is over, things don’t get any better. The vegetation covers everything, and I even have to walk the descents. After that comes a brutal up-and-down following the <em>route</em> of a gas pipeline, with a horrible surface and impossible slopes. This is definitely not what I was looking for. I don’t even feel like taking a photo, although, come to think of it, there isn’t much to photograph anyway. At last I spot the main road on the GPS and flee towards it. This road has a decent surface and very little traffic. I don’t see the point of crossing the hills pushing the bike. From here to the finish, I’ll stick to the road.

Back on the road, I make a pit stop to buy some food and fill up with water. From here to the finish, nothing worth mentioning.

The hotel in <em>Aranisi</em> surprises me. Modest on the outside, but beautifully kept inside, with excellent <em>WiFi</em>. I have a shower, lick my wounds and get ready for tomorrow’s stage, where I hope to see prettier places.
